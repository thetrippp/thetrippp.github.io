Technically, the parliamentary vote represents a fundamental reform of the nation's penal system, moving away from retributive justice towards a more rehabilitative or strictly punitive framework without the ultimate sanction. The fact that no executions had occurred since 2004 suggests a de facto moratorium was already in place, but the legal abolition provides a permanent and legally binding commitment, preventing future governments from reinstating it without another legislative overhaul. This move aligns Lebanon with a growing global trend towards the abolition of capital punishment.

Key Players & Motivations

The Lebanese Parliament, through its legislative vote, is primarily motivated by a commitment to human rights, legal reform, and aligning Lebanon with international norms. The Ripple Effect
