While indirect evidence from phenomena like gaps in protoplanetary disks has long pointed to the presence of forming planets, directly imaging a protoplanet while it is still actively accreting material from the surrounding gas has been an elusive holy grail for astronomers. ALMA, an international array of 66 high-precision antennas in the Atacama Desert, is uniquely suited for this task. This allows astronomers to map the kinematics of the gas, observing how it flows onto the forming planet.
